One story from the route: Lady Carcas

As you enter the Cité through the Porte Narbonnaise, look up: a stone bust of a woman stands at the entrance. This is Lady Carcas, the heroine of Carcassonne's best-known legend.

According to the legend, Charlemagne's army besieged the city for five years. When supplies ran out, the ruler, Carcas, had what was left brought to her: one pig and a sack of grain. The pig was fed the grain and thrown from the walls at the besiegers' feet. Seeing the defenders feed wheat to their livestock, Charlemagne concluded the city still had food for years and lifted the siege. Carcas ordered every bell to be rung, and one of the soldiers cried out: “Carcas sonne!” — “Carcas is ringing!” That, the legend says, is where the city's name comes from.

It's a lovely story, but it is only a legend. It was written down in the 16th century, and Lady Carcas herself was most likely invented. The name is much older: in Roman times the town was already known as Carcaso. The bust at the gate is a copy; the 16th-century original is kept in the castle.

There are nineteen stories like this along the route, from the Canal du Midi to the Porte d'Aude. Some are legends, like this one; others are the well-documented history of the fortress and the town.

The route: 8 stages, 19 stories

Below are the eight main stages of the walk. In the app the route is split into 19 audio stories, with extra stops and illustrations.

Schematic route map: 8 stages from the station to the Porte d'Aude. In the app the route has 19 stories.
  1. Train station and Canal du Midi — a 17th-century canal on the UNESCO list, with a lock a few steps from the station.
  2. Place Carnot — the heart of the lower town, the Bastide Saint-Louis founded in the 13th century: cafés, the Neptune fountain, the market.
  3. Museum of Fine Arts — a small municipal museum on the way to the fortress.
  4. Old Bridge (Pont Vieux) — a 14th-century bridge and your first big view of the fortress: both rings of walls, the towers and the castle.
  5. Porte Narbonnaise and the double walls — the main entrance to the Cité and the bust of Lady Carcas.
  6. Streets of the Cité — shops, museums and cafés inside the walls.
  7. Château Comtal (outside) and the Basilica of Saint-Nazaire — the Trencavel viscounts' castle and a basilica with 13th–14th-century stained glass, free to enter.
  8. Porte d'Aude — the quiet western exit and the walk down to the river.

The route doesn't cover every street in Carcassonne — it's the main line through the lower town and the fortress. What's free inside the walls and what needs a ticket is explained in our Carcassonne walls guide.

Did you know? Carcassonne gave its name to one of the world's best-known board games: Klaus-Jürgen Wrede's Carcassonne came out in 2000 and a year later won Germany's top board-game award, the Spiel des Jahres.

Good to know before you go

  • Start: Carcassonne train station (1 Av. du Maréchal Joffre) — but you can begin at any point on the route. No guide meets you there: this is a self-guided walk.
  • The last stretch is a climb up to the fortress: not long, but noticeable. Wear comfortable shoes: cobbles, slopes and steps.
  • At midday in summer the stone is hot and there's little shade — morning or late afternoon is better.
  • Tickets aren't included in the audio guide: the Château Comtal and the wall-walk are separate, €13 (Oct–Mar) / €19 (Apr–Sep), free for under 18s. You can book a timed ticket online via Tiqets; details are in our Château Comtal guide.
  • Back to the station: the same way over the Old Bridge, or by bus.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can you see Carcassonne on your own, without a tour?

Yes. The streets, gates and squares of the fortress, the lices between the walls and the basilica are free, and it's about two kilometres on foot from the station to the Cité. You only need a ticket for the Château Comtal and the walls.

How many stories are in the audio guide and how long does the walk take?

19 stories along the route from the station to the Porte d'Aude; the whole walk takes about two hours at your own pace. If you also visit the castle, add 45 minutes to an hour and a half.

Do I need internet?

Only to buy and download the tour. Once downloaded, the map and audio work offline.

Does the audio guide include a castle ticket?

No. The audio guide follows a free route; the Château Comtal and the wall-walk need a separate ticket.

Can I start somewhere other than the station?

Yes, you can start at any point. If you arrive by car or bus right at the fortress, start at the Porte Narbonnaise and pick the story you want from the list of stops; you can go back to the start of the route at any time.

Which languages is the audio guide in?

Eight: English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Polish and Russian.

Is the walk suitable for children?

Yes: the stories are short, you can pause at any moment, and children usually enjoy legends like Lady Carcas. With a stroller it's harder on the cobbles and the climb to the fortress.
