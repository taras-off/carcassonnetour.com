Château Comtal: Is Carcassonne's Castle Worth the Ticket?

Yes, if you want the ramparts, the view of the defences from above and the viscounts' castle; no, if the free Cité is enough. What the ticket includes, what's free, and how to do it well.

By Eugene · Updated September 2026

The short answer: yes, if you want to climb onto the ramparts, see Carcassonne's defensive system from above, and go inside the castle of the medieval viscounts. If walking the lanes of the Cité is enough for you, you can do that entirely for free. The Château Comtal is the one main paid sight inside the medieval city, and it's worth knowing exactly what you're paying for before you queue.

The frame is simple. Walking the Cité — its streets, squares, gates, the basilica and the corridor between the double walls — is free. The ticket buys what the free route doesn't: the viscounts' castle inside the city, and the walk along the top of the inner ramparts.

A little history, so you know what you're looking at. In the early 13th century the castle belonged to the Trencavel viscounts. In 1209, during the Albigensian Crusade, crusaders besieged Carcassonne; the young viscount Raymond-Roger Trencavel was taken prisoner and soon died in captivity. The city later passed to the French crown, which added the second ring of walls, and in the 19th century the architect Viollet-le-Duc restored it on a grand scale. So what you see today is both a medieval fortress and a monument of 19th-century restoration.

What the ticket includes

One ticket covers the Château Comtal — the Trencavel viscounts' castle inside the city — and the walk along the top of the inner ramparts. Inside the castle buildings there are rooms with a stone and archaeological exhibition (carved fragments, models and panels on the city's history and the Cathar period). Self-guided entry is standard; a live guided tour is an optional paid extra, best booked ahead. Plan on roughly 45 minutes to an hour and a half depending on how long you spend on the walls.

Is it worth it?

It depends on what you came for — but the castle's value is real. The ticket takes you up onto the wall-walk to see Carcassonne's defensive system from above: both rings of walls, the approaches, the lower town and the countryside — which the free city can't give you. Inside are the fortress architecture, the interior spaces and a history exhibition. Don't expect lavishly furnished royal apartments: what matters here is the fortress itself, the view of the defences and the rampart walk, not furniture. If you're mainly after atmosphere — the lanes and the floodlit walls at night — you can get almost all of that for free and skip the castle without feeling you missed the point.

How the visit works

The entrance to the paid area is inside the Cité, by the castle; the order of the visit and signage are on site. There's a ticket check and a security control at the entrance; in high season there can be a wait, and a timed ticket doesn't guarantee skipping the queue. You usually tour the castle rooms first, then go out onto the wall-walk; re-entry on a single ticket is generally not allowed, so plan to see it in one go. The rampart route involves stairs and uneven stone, so it's of limited suitability for strollers and visitors with reduced mobility; parts of the courtyard and rooms are more accessible. Check current accessibility and visit arrangements on the official site.

How long you need

Budget about 45 minutes to an hour for a relaxed self-guided visit, and up to an hour and a half if you walk the full open section of the ramparts and linger on the views. It's not a half-day attraction — which is why it fits neatly into a morning in the Cité.

When to go and how to book

In high season, choose the earliest available entry time or a later slot, leaving enough time for the whole rampart route. In low season you can usually buy at the gate. Book a live guided tour ahead (a paid extra, about an hour, in French and English) — slots are limited. And on price, honestly: the official box office is usually the cheapest; resellers (Tiqets and others) may charge the same or slightly more for the convenience of a mobile ticket bought in advance. Compare before you buy.

Free vs ticket — what you give up by skipping it

Without a ticket you still get a lot: all the streets and squares, both great gate-towers, the basilica, and long stretches of the lices, the grassy corridor between the two walls. Only the ticket gives you the castle itself and the upper wall-walk — the higher viewpoints and the double-wall defence seen from above. If budget is tight, the free city is a full experience; the ticket is the upgrade for the castle and the vantage.

The walls don't explain themselves

You can climb the ramparts and photograph every tower and still not grasp why the castle sits where it does, why one stretch of wall is Roman and the next medieval, or how close Carcassonne came to demolition before it was rebuilt in the 19th century.

FAQ

Château Comtal — questions

Is the Château Comtal worth visiting?

Worth it if you want to climb the ramparts, see the defensive system from above and go inside the viscounts' castle. If you're mainly after atmosphere, the free city covers most of it and you can skip the castle.

How much is the Château Comtal ticket?

€13 in low season (Oct–Mar) and €19 in high season (Apr–Sep). Free for under 18s, for 18–25-year-old EU/EEA nationals and residents, and on the first Sunday of the month from November to March. Check current prices and concessions on the official site, remparts-carcassonne.fr.

What's included in the ticket?

The Château Comtal, the walk along the top of the inner ramparts, and rooms with a stone and archaeological exhibition. A live guided tour is a separate paid extra.

How does the visit work and how long does it take?

Entry is inside the Cité, by the castle, with a check at the entrance. You usually see the castle rooms first, then the wall-walk; re-entry on one ticket is generally not allowed. Allow about 45 minutes to an hour and a half.

Is the castle accessible for strollers and visitors with reduced mobility?

The rampart route involves stairs and uneven stone, so it's of limited suitability; parts of the courtyard and rooms are more accessible. Check current conditions on the official site.

What can you see in Carcassonne for free?

Almost everything else: the streets, squares, both main gates, the basilica and long stretches of the lices between the double walls. Only the castle and the upper rampart walk need a ticket.
